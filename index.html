THE STORY BEHIND THE NAME

Remembering Archbishop Joseph Kiwanuka.

Joseph Nakabaale Kiwanuka is central to the identity of Kitovu. Masaka Diocese records that he was ordained a priest in 1929, appointed Bishop of Masaka in 1939, and moved the diocesan headquarters from Villa Maria to Kitovu in 1940. He later became Archbishop of Rubaga in 1961 and took part in the Second Vatican Council.

His legacy is especially meaningful for a school: faith, education, formation, service and the development of people who can contribute to society. Naming the school after him connects today's learners with that wider Ugandan and Catholic educational heritage rooted in Kitovu.

“The leadership of Archbishop Kiwánuka opened the door to the full development of the Catholic Church in Africa.”

HISTORY OF THE SCHOOL

A legacy rooted in faith, learning and Kitovu.

This history brings together the school’s Catholic setting, the story of its namesake and the development of Masaka Diocese. A precise school establishment year is not stated here because it needs confirmation from the school’s own records.

Archbishop Kiwanuka Secondary School stands within the Catholic educational tradition of Kitovu, Masaka. Its name honours Archbishop Joseph Nakabaale Kiwanuka, a pioneering African Catholic bishop whose life and ministry were closely connected with the growth of the Church in Uganda and the development of Kitovu as a diocesan centre.

Education in this setting is more than academic instruction. It brings together intellectual growth, faith, personal discipline, responsibility and service. The school’s motto — “Discipline for Knowledge and Wisdom” — expresses that combination: learners are encouraged to develop sound habits, seek knowledge and use what they learn wisely.

1891 · REGIONAL CONTEXT

Catholic mission in the Masaka region

The Diocese’s own history describes Catholic evangelisation in the area beginning in 1891 with the White Fathers. Over time, mission communities developed schools, pastoral services and other institutions that helped shape the region’s educational landscape.

1899 · THE SCHOOL’S NAMESAKE

The birth of Joseph Kiwanuka

Joseph Nakabaale Kiwanuka was born in 1899. His journey through Catholic education and seminary formation led him into a life of priestly service, scholarship and leadership at a time when African representation in senior Church leadership was uncommon.

1929 · PRIESTHOOD

Formation, scholarship and service

Kiwanuka was ordained a priest in 1929 and later pursued advanced studies in Rome. His academic formation and pastoral work helped prepare him for a leadership role that would become a landmark in the history of the Catholic Church in Africa.

1939 · EPISCOPAL MILESTONE

A pioneering African bishop

In 1939 Kiwanuka became Bishop of Masaka. His appointment and consecration marked an important milestone in the emergence of African leadership within the modern Roman Catholic hierarchy south of the Sahara.

1940 · KITOVU

Kitovu becomes a diocesan centre

Masaka Diocese records that the diocesan headquarters moved from Villa Maria to Kitovu in 1940. This strengthened Kitovu’s place in diocesan life and connected the area with a wider network of Catholic pastoral, educational and community institutions.

1953 · DIOCESAN DEVELOPMENT

The Diocese of Masaka is formally established

Masaka Diocese came into being through the Apostolic Constitution Quemadmodum ad Nos of Pope Pius XII on 25 March 1953. Joseph Kiwanuka was transferred to the new diocese, linking his episcopal leadership directly with its early history.

1961–1965 · WIDER CHURCH

Archbishop and participant in Vatican II

Kiwanuka became Archbishop of Rubaga in 1961 and took part in the Second Vatican Council. His story remains part of Uganda’s wider religious and educational heritage, remembered for leadership, learning and service.

LEADERSHIP & CATHOLIC EDUCATION

Guided by faith and service.

The school belongs to the educational family of the Catholic Diocese of Masaka. These profiles recognise the diocesan shepherd and the school’s head teacher.

Bishop of Masaka Diocese

Rt. Rev. Serverus Jjumba

Bishop Serverus Jjumba has served as the Ordinary of Masaka Diocese since his episcopal consecration and installation on 6 July 2019. Born in Bulando, Kitovu, he studied at local Catholic schools before seminary formation and theological studies.

Before becoming bishop, he served as a teacher and bursar at Bukalasa Minor Seminary, diocesan treasurer and Vicar General. As head of the Diocese, he provides pastoral leadership to its Catholic community, including its schools.

Head Teacher

Fr. Herman Joseph Kisekka

Fr. Herman Joseph Kisekka is the school’s head teacher. Published reporting by the Daily Monitor identified him as headmaster of Archbishop Kiwanuka Secondary School, Kitovu, in its 2019 coverage of the appointment of Bishop Serverus Jjumba.

OUR TEACHING STAFF

Dedicated educators, one shared purpose.

The school’s teaching staff support academic achievement, personal formation and the development of responsible citizens, in keeping with the school motto: “Discipline for Knowledge and Wisdom.”

A trained, committed and collaborative team

The trained teaching staff of Archbishop Kiwanuka Secondary School Kitovu in Masaka are dedicated to academic excellence and the development of the whole learner. As educators at a Catholic-founded institution under the Masaka Diocese, they are expected to model moral and ethical values while guiding students in knowledge, discipline, responsibility and service.

Across Ordinary and Advanced Level learning, teachers guide learners through their respective subject areas, spanning the Arts and Sciences. They support Uganda’s curriculum through lessons, practical work, discussion, skill-building and active participation. Science learning is strengthened by practical sessions, while ICT and other applied subjects help learners connect classroom knowledge with useful skills.

Teaching and academic guidance

Teachers bring subject knowledge and classroom experience to help learners understand concepts, ask questions, practise new skills and prepare for Uganda National Examinations Board (UNEB) assessments. They monitor progress, offer guidance beyond the lesson and work with school leadership to support educational goals.

  • Teaching across a broad range of Arts and Science subjects.
  • Supporting both Ordinary and Advanced Level learners.
  • Encouraging practical work, active participation and independent thinking.
  • Promoting professional growth and effective teaching methods.

Character, care and school community

Teachers contribute to a welcoming environment for both day and boarding students. They encourage respect, inclusivity, spiritual growth and responsible conduct, and work with parents and guardians to support learner welfare and academic progress. Through co-curricular activities, cultural performances, talent showcases and friendly sporting events, staff help learners discover their abilities and build confidence.

  • Integrating Christian values, prayer and character formation into school life.
  • Mentoring student leaders and encouraging creativity and participation.
  • Building constructive relationships with families and the wider community.
  • Promoting high aspirations and preparation for life beyond school.

Languages, practical learning and wider opportunities

In addition to core subjects, the school’s wider learning environment includes languages such as German, helping learners build communication skills and broaden their horizons. Teachers encourage learners to connect knowledge with everyday life, collaborate with others and develop the confidence to contribute positively to Uganda’s future. Current subject combinations, facilities and programme details should be confirmed with the school for each academic year.
